Cover Letter Template for Graphic Designer That Passes ATS

Updated 31 July 2026 · 2 min read
A graphic designer cover letter needs four short paragraphs: why this specific company, the one result that proves you can do the job, how your experience maps to their stated requirements, and a direct close. One page, never more.

To increase your chances of getting an interview as a graphic designer, your cover letter needs to pass through applicant tracking systems (ATS) and catch the eye of the hiring manager. A well-structured cover letter template can help you achieve this. Here's what you need to include:

Essential Sections

  • Introduction: Start with a formal greeting, addressing the hiring manager by title and last name (if possible), and clearly state the position you're applying for.
  • Summary: Briefly highlight your relevant experience, skills, and achievements as a graphic designer, using keywords from the job description.
  • Body: Divide this section into 2-3 paragraphs, each focusing on a specific aspect of your experience, skills, or education that aligns with the job requirements.
  • Closing: End with a professional closing, such as a thank you note, and include your contact information (email address and phone number).

ATS-Friendly Formatting

  • Use a standard font, such as Arial, Calibri or Helvetica, in size 10 or 11 points.
  • Set your margins to 0.5-1 inch on all sides to ensure your content fits within the ATS scanning area.
  • Use bullet points (•) instead of dashes or asterisks to list your skills or achievements.
  • Avoid using tables, images, or excessive formatting, as these can confuse the ATS algorithms.
  • Save your cover letter in a widely accepted format, such as .docx or .pdf.

Keyword Optimization

  • Identify the most relevant keywords and phrases from the job description, such as Adobe Creative Suite, visual design, or branding.
  • Incorporate these keywords naturally into your cover letter, especially in your summary and body sections.
  • Use variations of the keywords to avoid repetition and demonstrate your understanding of the skills and concepts.
  • Include relevant software, tools, or technologies, such as Sketch, Figma, or InDesign, to showcase your technical skills.

Common questions

How many keywords should a graphic designer resume include?

Aim for the 12-15 hard skills that appear across multiple postings for the same title. Padding beyond that reads as keyword stuffing to both parsers and humans.

Does an ATS reject a graphic designer resume automatically?

Most applicant tracking systems rank and filter rather than reject outright, but a low keyword match means a human may never see the resume. The parser is matching strings, not judging experience.

Should I use a PDF or a Word document?

Use .docx unless the posting explicitly asks for PDF. Older parsers still handle Word more reliably.
