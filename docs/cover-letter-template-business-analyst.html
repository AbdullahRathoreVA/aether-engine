Cover Letter Template for Business Analyst That Passes ATS

Updated 31 July 2026 · 2 min read
A business analyst cover letter needs four short paragraphs: why this specific company, the one result that proves you can do the job, how your experience maps to their stated requirements, and a direct close. One page, never more.

To increase your chances of getting an interview for a business analyst position, you need a cover letter that not only highlights your skills and experience but also passes through applicant tracking systems (ATS). A well-crafted cover letter template can help you achieve this. Here's a breakdown of what you should include in your cover letter to make it more effective.

Essential Sections and Keywords

  • Introduction: Start with a professional greeting, addressing the hiring manager by title and last name (if possible), and include the job title and reference number (if provided).
  • Summary/Objective: Briefly outline your experience, qualifications, and career goals as a business analyst, using keywords from the job posting such as data analysis, process improvement, and stakeholder management.
  • Body: Divide the body into paragraphs focusing on your relevant experience, skills, and achievements, incorporating keywords like business intelligence tools, agile methodologies, and cost-benefit analysis.

ATS-Friendly Formatting and Content

  • Use a standard font such as Arial, Calibri, or Helvetica, and a font size between 10 and 12 points to ensure readability and compatibility with ATS.
  • Save your cover letter in a .docx or .pdf format to prevent formatting issues during the upload process.
  • Avoid using tables, images, or excessive formatting, as these can hinder the ATS from properly parsing your cover letter.
  • Incorporate relevant business analyst certifications, such as CBAP or CCBA, to demonstrate your expertise and commitment to the field.

Customization and Final Checks

  • Tailor your cover letter to each job application by highlighting the skills and experiences that match the job requirements, and use language from the job posting to describe your qualifications.
  • Use specific examples to demonstrate your skills and accomplishments, such as increased efficiency by 25% or improved sales by 15%, to make your application more compelling.
  • Proofread your cover letter multiple times for grammar, spelling, and punctuation errors, and ask a friend or mentor to review it for feedback and suggestions.
  • Use online tools or consult with a career advisor to test your cover letter's ATS compatibility and make necessary adjustments before submitting your application.

Common questions

How many keywords should a business analyst resume include?

Aim for the 12-15 hard skills that appear across multiple postings for the same title. Padding beyond that reads as keyword stuffing to both parsers and humans.

Does an ATS reject a business analyst resume automatically?

Most applicant tracking systems rank and filter rather than reject outright, but a low keyword match means a human may never see the resume. The parser is matching strings, not judging experience.

Should I use a PDF or a Word document?

Use .docx unless the posting explicitly asks for PDF. Older parsers still handle Word more reliably.
